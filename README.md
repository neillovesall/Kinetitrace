KineTrace

KineTrace is a flexible wearable motion tracking system that uses two IMU sensors to measure and analyze joint movement in real time. It is designed for rehabilitation, physical therapy, and general biomechanics tracking outside of clinical environments.

After injury or surgery, people often have to complete rehab exercises at home without direct supervision. This makes it hard to know if movements are being done correctly. KineTrace solves this by tracking motion between two connected body segments and turning it into clear, real-time feedback.

The system is not limited to one joint. The two sensors can be placed on any connected segments of the body, such as thigh and shin for knee tracking or upper arm and forearm for elbow tracking. This makes the system adaptable to different use cases in biomechanics and sports science.

How it works
Two IMU sensors collect motion data from connected body segments
Sensor fusion and calibration are used to ensure accurate orientation tracking
The system computes relative joint angles in real time
Movement quality is analyzed and displayed through live feedback and scoring
Why it matters

KineTrace helps users understand whether they are performing movements correctly during rehabilitation. Instead of guessing or relying on occasional clinic visits, users get immediate feedback they can act on. This can improve consistency, reduce incorrect movement habits, and support better recovery outcomes.

Potential applications
Physical rehabilitation
Sports training
Biomechanics research
Human movement analysis
Status

This project is an ongoing engineering and research system exploring low-cost wearable sensing and real-time motion analysis.
